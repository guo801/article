\begin{abstract}
The comprehensive characterization of novel materials and the execution of standardized biological assays are heavily bottlenecked by manual, error-prone laboratory workflows involving multi-step solution preparation, surface coating, and serial dilution. While robotic automation offers a promising solution, existing single-arm systems rely on rigid physical teaching, lack the flexibility to safely handle corrosive liquid reagents across spatially distributed workstations, and cannot coordinate the heterogeneous manipulation demands of chemical synthesis and biological preparation within a unified framework. Moreover, reliable inter-platform container handover during multi-stage workflows remains an open challenge. To address these challenges, we present a digital twin-driven multi-platform hybrid robotic framework tailored for the end-to-end automation of bio-chemical laboratory workflows, including precision solution preparation, anti-corrosion thin film fabrication on metal substrates, and gradient serial dilution for microbial culture. Our system comprises a 7-DoF Franka Panda arm for precision liquid handling and solution mixing at the preparation workstation, a differential-drive mobile robot with a stabilization-optimized tray for inter-station transport, and a 6-DoF UR3 arm for surface coating operations and automated serial dilution at the analysis workstation. Built upon a high-fidelity, GPU-accelerated NVIDIA Isaac Sim ecosystem, the framework establishes unified digital twins for all three platforms. To handle the heterogeneous manipulation requirements across these distinct workflows, we propose a macro-micro decoupled control strategy: macroscopic inter-station transport is governed by constraint-aware classical planning with minimum-jerk trajectory optimization and dynamic velocity bounds to eliminate liquid sloshing and splashing; precision-critical manipulations---including reagent pipetting, thin film dip-coating, and micro-volume serial dilution---are executed by platform-specific Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and Imitation Learning (IL) policies trained via massively parallel simulation with domain randomization. Furthermore, we introduce a vision-guided inter-platform handover protocol with ArUco marker-based localization, docking confirmation signals, and force-sensor verification to ensure reliable container transfer. For task-level orchestration, we use a failure-aware hierarchical state machine with a reusable skill library and explicit transition and recovery guards. Experiments on the evaluated laboratory tasks demonstrate that our framework achieves an 82.7\% pooled success rate across the evaluated manipulation-skill trials, significantly reducing physical trial-and-error costs and providing a highly scalable foundation for next-generation self-driving laboratories.
\end{abstract}
