\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The comprehensive evaluation of functional materials and the execution of standardized biological assays---spanning surface wettability, corrosion resistance, anti-microbial activity, and bio-reactivity---are crucial for advancing biomedical engineering, materials science, and biotechnology. Traditionally, these multi-stage experimental workflows require labor-intensive, manual laboratory procedures. For instance, preparing a single anti-corrosion thin film sample involves sequential solution preparation with precise reagent ratios, controlled surface coating on metal substrates, and subsequent characterization through contact angle measurement or electrochemical testing. Similarly, establishing minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) profiles for antimicrobial agents requires meticulous serial dilution across 96-well microplates followed by extended culture incubation. These manual workloads are not only tedious and throughput-limited, but also highly susceptible to human error, cross-contamination, and liquid spillage, severely compromising experimental reproducibility and operational safety---particularly when handling corrosive or biohazardous reagents.

Although robotic automation has begun to transform laboratory operations, its adoption in complex multi-workflow experimental pipelines remains constrained by several fundamental limitations. First, existing automated workcells typically rely on rigid physical teaching and pre-programmed waypoints, which, while effective for simple repetitive transport, lack the dynamic adaptability required for the diverse manipulation demands of heterogeneous workflows---such as precision micro-pipetting during solution preparation, controlled dip-coating velocity during thin film fabrication, and micro-volume liquid handling during serial dilution. Second, most deployed systems employ a single robotic arm, limiting throughput and spatial coverage when complex workflows require operations at multiple physically separated workstations. Third, when multiple heterogeneous platforms (e.g., distinct manipulators at separate workstations connected by autonomous mobile transport) must collaborate, reliable container handover between platforms introduces a critical failure point that has received insufficient attention in the existing literature. Fourth, coordinating heterogeneous skills across long workflows requires explicit completion checks and recovery from failed stages. Finally, debugging hard-coded robotic trajectories directly in physical laboratories involving corrosive thin film precursors or biohazardous microbial cultures is exceptionally risky and expensive.

To address these challenges, we present a digital twin-driven multi-platform hybrid robotic framework for bio-chemical laboratory workflows, including precision solution preparation with controlled reagent mixing ratios, anti-corrosion thin film fabrication on metal substrates via dip-coating, and gradient serial dilution for microbial culture in 96-well microplates. Our system architecture consists of three physically separated yet digitally unified robotic platforms (Fig.~\ref{fig:overall_framework}):

\textbf{Franka Panda Arm (Station A --- Solution Preparation):} A 7-DoF manipulator equipped with joint torque sensing and impedance control, responsible for grasping reagent bottles, precision volumetric pipetting, controlled pouring for solution mixing, and placing prepared solutions onto the mobile robot's transport tray.

\textbf{Differential-Drive Mobile Robot (Inter-Station Transport):} An autonomous mobile platform equipped with a stabilization-optimized transport tray featuring anti-slip silicone padding and ArUco fiducial markers, responsible for spill-free transit of open liquid containers between the preparation station and the analysis station.

\textbf{UR3 Arm (Station B --- Surface Coating and Biological Assay):} A compact 6-DoF manipulator fitted with a Robotiq 2F-85 parallel gripper, responsible for retrieving containers from the mobile tray, executing controlled dip-coating operations on metal substrates for anti-corrosion thin film fabrication, and performing automated serial dilution across 96-well microplates for microbial culture preparation.

Built within the high-fidelity NVIDIA Isaac Sim ecosystem, our framework establishes GPU-accelerated virtual counterparts for all three platforms, enabling risk-free policy learning and pre-deployment verification across thousands of parallel simulated environments. Crucially, we propose a macro-micro decoupled control strategy to handle the heterogeneous task requirements. For macroscopic inter-station transport, we formulate the mobile robot's navigation as a constrained classical optimization problem with minimum-jerk trajectory optimization and dynamic velocity bounds derived from container geometry and fill ratio, fundamentally eliminating liquid sloshing and spillage risks. For precision-critical manipulations at each workstation, we implement platform-specific Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and Imitation Learning (IL) pipelines:

For solution preparation (Panda arm), we train posture-aware PPO agents for upright grasping and implement IL-based pipetting policies with regularized few-shot real-world fine-tuning to achieve microliter-level volumetric accuracy. For thin film fabrication (UR3 arm), we develop DRL policies that learn to control dip-coating speed profiles and substrate immersion angles, optimizing for coating uniformity through reward functions that penalize film thickness variation. For serial dilution (UR3 arm), we train IL policies for sequential multi-well pipetting with automated tip-changing behaviors, ensuring consistent dilution ratios while preventing inter-well cross-contamination.

Furthermore, to ensure reliable inter-platform container handover, we introduce a vision-guided handover protocol integrating ArUco marker-based tray localization, structured docking confirmation signal sequences, and force-sensor verification at the gripper. For task-level orchestration, we use a failure-aware hierarchical state machine and a reusable Skill Primitive Library with standardized input-output interfaces. Predefined guards based on observed task conditions determine skill transitions and recovery actions.

In summary, the current system contributions and one proposed extension are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multi-Workflow Digital Twin Ecosystem:} We construct a unified high-fidelity digital twin in Isaac Sim encompassing the robotic platforms and laboratory workflows, enabling parallelized policy training without physical risk.
    \item \textbf{Online Twin Loop (Proposed Extension):} We specify a physical-to-virtual loop that time-aligns multimodal robot and laboratory states, calibrates identifiable model parameters from twin--real residuals, and gates physical actions using uncertainty-aware twin predictions. This module is presented with a validation protocol; the current experimental success rates do not establish its benefit.
    \item \textbf{Macro-Micro Decoupled Hybrid Control for Heterogeneous Workflows:} We propose a control architecture that delegates macroscopic mobile robot transport to constraint-aware classical planning, while leveraging platform-specific DRL and IL policies tailored to the distinct manipulation demands of chemical synthesis, surface coating, and biological preparation.
    \item \textbf{Vision-Guided Inter-Platform Handover Protocol:} We introduce a structured container handover mechanism addressing the critical challenge of reliable multi-platform container transfer in laboratory automation.
    \item \textbf{Hierarchical Skill Orchestration:} We construct a reusable Skill Primitive Library sequenced by a failure-aware hierarchical state machine, with explicit completion, timeout, and recovery conditions.
\end{itemize}
